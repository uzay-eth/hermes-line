# R1 delta 10 — owner line authentication

**Verdict: NO** for the attended ConversationRelay trial in which the owner enters a PIN, requests a call to his Fi line and asks to be bridged in. Fix N12–N14 before this new authentication/approval path is used; fix the small N15 timer defect alongside them. No Critical finding. N12 is High; N13/N14 are Medium; N15 is Low.

Reviewed **5b654a4**, specifically `git diff 2faacc9 5b654a4 -- plugin/ tests/l1`, with L1 sections 21–22. References below are to that exact revision exported into this review's `disposable/`, not the older checked-out worktree. This is a focused delta, not a new full R1 certification.

## Findings

### N12 — High: a spoken yes authorizes a model-selected destination before read-back

**Evidence:** `plugin/line/engine/machine.py:742–765` sets `pending_confirm` immediately inside the tool handler and saves `_readback` for later. `_on_speech` at lines 440–443 accepts approval whenever that pending object exists. `_owner_reply` at lines 767–780 creates the ticket without checking whether read-back was sent or heard. Only `_after_turn` at lines 790–793 sends the fixed confirmation. `service.py:625–650` binds the resulting ticket to task/destination, but cannot establish that the owner heard either.

**Failure:** after PIN verification, Hermes invokes the new-task tool while its reply is still running. The owner says “yes” to an earlier question, or queued/injected audio contains “yes.” The line approves the model-selected task before announcing its number. Replacing a pending task creates the same interval for the replacement number. A model mistake or injection can therefore acquire destination approval without an informed read-back. Existing destination policy still limits the number; this is a consent bypass, not an emergency-number bypass.

**Reproduction:** `fixtures/probe_owner.py` invokes the real in-call tool on a verified session, confirms `_readback` is still pending and no “To confirm” has been sent, then sends “Yes.” through the relay. A voice approval ticket is created while the read-back remains unsent. No real provider is used.

**Smallest fix:** separate proposed and confirmable requests. Arm a confirmation for a particular task/destination/generation only after the fixed read-back completes; reject speech queued before that point, invalidate on interruption/replacement and expire the confirmation. Do not treat arbitrary later affirmatives after unrelated conversation as confirmation. Test delayed model completion, barge-in, queued yes, replacement and expiry.

**Coverage gap:** `test_R13_a_yes_without_a_read_back_approves_nothing` tests yes with no pending task, not a pending task whose read-back has not happened. Other tests deliberately wait for read-back before sending yes.

### N13 — Medium: three attempts is only a per-call limit; redial resets it

**Evidence:** `machine.py:120–125` initializes `pin_failures = 0` for each CallSession; lines 730–740 only end that call after three failures. `service.py:1245–1253` offers a new PIN phase whenever a newly admitted caller ID equals the owner number. No persistent attempt counter, cross-call cooldown or owner alert is installed by these changes. The `owner_pin_locked` event records a call-local result, not an account lockout.

**Failure:** a caller capable of presenting the owner's caller ID can make successive valid provider calls and try three guesses on each. Caller-ID spoofing does not require forging Twilio webhooks. With a supported four-digit PIN there are only 10,000 candidates, and the authentication oracle has no cumulative limit. Single-call admission serializes attempts but does not bound them over time.

**Reproduction:** three failed attempts, end of first call, then a new CallSid with the same owner number: the new session has zero failures and an active PIN prompt; verification succeeds with the fixture PIN. No bulk guessing was performed.

**Smallest fix:** persist a bounded attempt budget/cooldown for the owner credential/line across calls and process restarts, not merely a caller-controlled identity. Add bounded owner notification for lockout and an authenticated recovery path. Longer PINs alone do not replace throttling. Tests must cover redial and restart. The current three-failure test covers only one call.

### N14 — Medium: the reusable PIN can reach the transcript and Hermes after an ordinary greeting

**Evidence:** `machine.py:700–708` turns PIN entry off on any utterance without digits. Lines 440–445 then stop intercepting PIN speech and send it through generic masking. `plugin/line/engine/redact.py:33–72` masks short digit/word sequences only near a code/PIN keyword; it does not register or scrub the configured owner PIN as a secret value.

**Failure:** the owner hears the PIN prompt, first says “Hello,” then says the PIN as four digit words. The first utterance silently leaves PIN mode. The next utterance is persisted and passed to Hermes as ordinary business speech. Repeating a PIN after successful authentication has the same exposure boundary. This is a reusable authentication credential in transcript/model history, with potential propagation to snapshots, summaries and exported evidence that consume those records. I directly confirmed transcript and driver exposure; I did not separately generate every downstream artifact.

**Reproduction:** “Hello”, followed by the synthetic PIN “four eight two one”: the exact phrase appears in the store transcript and the Hermes driver's turns. Existing tests assert secrecy only while the PIN phase remains active; the stranger test even sends the PIN outside that phase without checking for disclosure.

**Smallest fix:** do not silently abandon secure entry on a preliminary greeting; use an explicit exit choice. Scrub the configured PIN and supported spoken forms at all persistence/model boundaries throughout the call, including retries/repeats, without logging it during registration. DTMF-only entry is a narrower alternative if spoken entry cannot be protected. Add skipped-phase, repeated-entry, partial-transcription and downstream-export tests.

### N15 — Low: pause-based PIN submission cancels its own hang-up

**Evidence:** `_pin_pause` at `machine.py:695–698` calls `_pin_attempt` from the stored timer task. `_pin_attempt` cancels that same task at lines 715–717. On a third failure, cancellation reaches the sleep before hang-up at lines 739–740, so the hang-up never runs.

**Reproduction:** three pause-triggered wrong attempts leave `pin_failures == 3`, `pin_phase == False`, the final timer cancelled and the call nonterminal. The probe shortens only the pause delay. This does not grant owner power or enable a fourth PIN attempt on that call, but contradicts the promised lockout teardown and can leave the call running until another timeout.

**Smallest fix:** do not cancel `asyncio.current_task()` when clearing the PIN timer; ensure the final failure owns a cancellation-safe hang-up. Test the pause branch, not just `#`, eight digits or speech.

## Authentication and consent assessment

`HERMES_LINE_OWNER_PIN` is declared optional and secret in `plugin/plugin.yaml:46–51`. `service.py:447–453` reads it through the injected secret getter and fails closed on unavailable/invalid values. `owner_line.py:19–33` accepts only 4–8 ASCII digits. No new PIN database or plaintext configuration copy is introduced. This review did not read any actual secret storage.

`owner_line.py:59–61` uses `hmac.compare_digest`, avoiding a prefix-dependent Python comparison. Incorrect guesses receive the same retry wording; only attempt counts and lock events are explicitly recorded. The helper comment about never revealing length is stronger than compare_digest's general guarantees for variable-length input; I found no practical remote timing exploit here. Entry grouping itself exposes protocol timing (eight digits versus `#`/pause), not secret-prefix correctness. PIN speech necessarily reaches the telephony transcription provider before plugin interception; the source cannot promise provider-side non-retention.

Caller ID alone does not set `owner_verified`. A normal stranger is not offered the prompt, and an owner-number caller without the PIN cannot use `owner_new_task`. A spoofed owner number can reach the prompt: N13 is therefore relevant. Missing/invalid PIN or media-stream transport cannot verify the caller. The ordinary configured inbound owner-ring permission remains available to unknown callers; it is a bounded ring of the configured owner, not authentication or permission to choose a number.

After a valid yes, tickets are process-local, single-use and bound to task plus canonical destination (`service.py:625–650,785–791`). They do not persist across restart. They are not bound to a read-back-completion generation or expiry (N12). Tool output cannot itself set `owner_verified` or submit a valid owner-voice ticket. The later outbound leg gets a fresh CallSession with `owner_verified=False`; its callee saying yes cannot approve another task. Audio injected into the already verified inbound session is treated as owner speech: PIN authenticates the session, not every subsequent speaker. Existing ingress tokens/REST checks still matter, and fallback ingress's prior limitations remain.

Dialing waits until the inbound call ends; it does **not** bridge the original inbound socket straight into a business call. `bring_owner=True` selects owner mode. `_approve` issues destination-bound dial and owner-ring grants (`service.py:864–888`); the final dial gate remains in `_dial`. The owner is subsequently rung at the configured owner number. `machine.py:907–945` consumes the ring grant through the destination gate before provider placement and presents the press-1 whisper. The new task cannot nominate a different owner-ring destination. Existing OTP-off requirements remain necessary.

## Round 12

The owner greeting/context now distinguishes caller ID from verified ownership (`service.py:409–418,455–465`), and round 13 suppresses the “most likely owner” note during PIN entry. Greeting alone grants no new-task permission. Name/business hints are text-only recognition hints (`service.py:471–488`); they include words from open tasks in unmatched inbound TwiML, not secrets or authorization. No side-effect gate consumes those hints.

`acp_driver.py:238–260` builds deferred tool names only from the narrowly parsed session toolset reply. `machine.py:281–287` adds that note after driver start/scope validation. This does not expand the tool set or bypass dispatch. The note names finish, digits and bring-owner; the new-task tool remains separately registered and protected by the verified-session check. The restored inbound ring grant is still conditional on `inbound_ring_owner`, destination-bound and single-use (`service.py:431–445`), with press-1 required for connection. No separate round-12 authorization bypass was found.
